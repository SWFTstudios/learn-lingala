How it works

Five minutes a day. A method built on how people actually learn languages.

No grammar lectures and no endless vocabulary lists. Each daily session moves you through the same four steps that research on language learning keeps pointing to.

  1. 1

    Learn whole phrases

    Fluent speakers rely on ready-made chunks like Likambo te and Kaka bongo. You learn the phrases people really say, each one in a real-life dialogue.

  2. 2

    Crack the patterns

    Pattern drills turn Nasali into Nakosala, “I” into “we”, a friend into an elder. You learn the rule once, then build sentences you've never seen.

  3. 3

    Use it in conversation

    Role-play the market, a taxi, a family visit. Then chat live with AI partners who reply in Lingala, correct you gently and give you a mission to complete.

  4. 4

    Never forget it

    Spaced repetition brings each phrase back right before you'd forget it: tomorrow, then in 3 days, a week, a month. What you learn stays learned.

Try it right now

Lingala is easier than you think.

Verbs carry their subject as a prefix: na- is “I”, to- is “we”. Add te to say “not”. Pick the pieces and you're already making real sentences. This is the engine of the whole language.

The path to conversational

A clear plan from zero to real conversations in about three months.

Five levels mapped to the international CEFR scale. Each one ends with things you can actually do, not chapters you've finished. Times assume about 10 minutes a day.

  1. 1Weeks 1–3 · A0 → A1

    Arrive Survival Lingala

    • Greet anyone and ask how they are
    • Say you don't understand, ask people to slow down
    • Count, ask prices and haggle at the market
  2. 2Weeks 3–6 · A1

    Get around Food, taxis & directions

    • Order food and say you're hungry
    • Take a taxi and give directions
    • Ask who, what, where, when and why
  3. 3Weeks 6–10 · A1 → A2

    Daily life Family, actions & feelings

    • Talk about your family
    • Say what you're doing and what you will do
    • Say what you want, can and can't do
  4. 4Weeks 10–14 · A2 → B1

    Conversations Past, plans & small talk

    • Tell what happened yesterday or long ago
    • Make plans and invite people out
    • Hold a 5-minute small-talk conversation
    You're conversational
  5. 5Ongoing · B1

    Mwana mboka Sound like a local

    • React naturally: “Ya solo?”, “Kaka bongo”
    • Use everyday Kinshasa street expressions
    • Show respect to elders the Congolese way

Questions

Good questions, honest answers.

How fast will I be able to have a conversation?

The course is designed to get you to small-talk conversations (Level 4, around A2–B1) in about three months at 10 minutes a day. Everyone's pace differs, so the app shows your own estimated date based on how often you practise.

Is it really free?

Yes. You can start right now, with no card and no trial timer. Lessons, reviews and the phrasebook work without an account. Create a free account to save your progress across devices and to use live chat and AI translation.

Which Lingala does it teach?

Everyday Lingala as it's spoken in Kinshasa, including the French words Kinois mix in, plus respectful forms for elders. Usage varies between Kinshasa, Brazzaville and other regions, and the app points out common variants.

Can I learn from French instead of English?

Yes. Choose English or French when you start, and switch any time in your profile. All meanings, grammar notes, dialogues, chat feedback and translations follow your choice.

Is there audio?

Native-speaker audio is on the way. For now, every lesson has you say phrases out loud, and the pronunciation guide teaches the sounds (Lingala is spelled the way it sounds, which helps a lot). The translator accepts your voice in English or French.

How accurate are the AI chat and translations?

The course phrases are written and checked by hand. Chat partners and AI translations are powered by Claude and told to use only Lingala they're confident in, and every translation shows a confidence level. AI can still make mistakes, so check anything surprising with a native speaker.

I already speak some Lingala. Will I be bored?

No. Take a level checkpoint and test out. Score 80% and you skip ahead, with those phrases added to your reviews so they stay fresh.
