Lesson 1

The Alphabet & Sounds

Good news: Lingala spelling is phonetic — words are pronounced the way they are written, every time. Master these sounds once and you can read anything.

The 7 vowels

Lingala has seven vowels. Five look familiar, and two — ɛ and ɔ — are "open" versions of e and o. In everyday writing (texts, social media) people usually just write e and o for all of them, but the sounds stay distinct in speech.

"ay" as in say (no glide)

"ee" as in see

O o
"o" as in go (no glide)
moto — person
Ɔ ɔ
"aw" as in law
matɔ́ndɔ — thank you

The special pairs (prenasalized consonants)

This is the most distinctive part of Lingala sounds. These letter pairs count as single sounds and can start a word — say the nasal (m/n) and the consonant together in one beat: mbote is "mbo-te", not "em-bo-te".

mb
mbote — hello
mp
mpo na — for, because of
nd
ndako — house
ng
ngai — I, me
nk
nkómbó — name
ns
nsoso — chicken
nt
ntango — time
nz
nzala — hunger
ny
like Spanish ñ
nyama — animal, meat
About tones: Lingala is a tonal language — the accent mark in matɔ́ndɔ shows a high tone. Textbooks write them, everyday writing usually doesn't. Don't stress about tones yet; you'll pick them up by listening.
