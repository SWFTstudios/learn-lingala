Lesson 2

Basic Sentence Structure

Lingala sentences follow the same order as English: Subject – Verb – Object. The twist is that the subject usually lives inside the verb as a prefix. Learn six prefixes and one verb pattern, and you can already say a lot.

1. Word order: Subject – Verb – Object

Mama alingi mwana. Mama (mother) + alingi (loves) + mwana (child) The mother loves the child.
Nalingi lipa. Na-lingi (I-love/want) + lipa (bread) I want bread.

2. The pronouns

These are the standalone pronouns — used for emphasis, after verbs, and after words like na (with/and):

LingalaEnglishExample
ngaiI, meNgai, nalingi kolia. — Me, I want to eat.
yoyou (one person)Nalingi yo. — I love you.
yehe, sheNamoni ye. — I see him/her.
bisowe, usYaka na biso. — Come with us.
binoyou (several people)Mbote na bino! — Hello to you all!
bangothey, themNayebi bango. — I know them.

3. The subject lives inside the verb

Dictionary verbs start with ko- (like English "to"): kolinga = to love/want, kolia = to eat, kozala = to be, koloba = to speak. To use a verb, drop ko- and add a subject prefix:

PrefixWhokolinga → “love / want”
na-Inalingi — I love / I want
o-youolingi — you love / you want
a-he / shealingi — he/she loves
to-wetolingi — we love / we want
bo-you allbolingi — you all love
ba-theybalingi — they love / they want

The -i ending marks the present. So the formula for now-statements is:

subject prefix + verb stem + -i

Nazali malamu. na- (I) + zal (be) + -i → “I am” + malamu (well) I am fine.
Tolingi mai. to- (we) + ling (want) + -i + mai (water) We want water.
Balobi Lingala. ba- (they) + lob (speak) + -i + Lingala They speak Lingala.

4. Saying no: wrap it with “te”

Negation is easy — keep the sentence exactly the same and put te at the end:

Nalingi lipa. I want bread.
Nalingi lipa te. I don't want bread.
Nayebi te. I don't know.

5. Asking questions

For yes/no questions, just raise your voice at the end — no word changes needed. Question words usually go where the answer would go:

Ozali malamu? Are you well? (How are you?)
Olingi nini? o- (you) + lingi (want) + nini (what) What do you want?
Ozali wapi? Where are you?
